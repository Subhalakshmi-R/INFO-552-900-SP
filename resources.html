Seasonal Pet Tips for Philadelphia

Philadelphia's weather changes significantly across the four seasons. Here is what to keep in mind for your pet throughout the year.

Spring Spring is a great time to get outside, but watch for seasonal hazards. Many common spring plants such as tulips, daffodils, and azaleas are toxic to dogs and cats. Keep your pet away from mulched garden beds, which can contain chemicals or mold. Spring also brings fleas and ticks - talk to your vet about prevention before the season starts.

ASPCA - Toxic and Non-Toxic Plants
Summer Philadelphia summers can be hot and humid. Avoid walking your dog on asphalt during peak heat hours - pavement can burn paw pads. Always bring fresh water on walks. Never leave your pet in a parked car, even briefly. Watch for signs of heatstroke including heavy panting, drooling, or weakness, and contact a vet immediately if you notice these signs.

Philadelphia Parks and Recreation - Dog Regulations
Fall Fall brings fallen leaves and cooler temperatures. Leaf piles can hide sharp objects, animal waste, or insects. Mushrooms that grow in damp fall conditions can be toxic if eaten. As the weather cools, keep an eye on older pets who may be more sensitive to temperature changes. This is also a good time to schedule a routine vet checkup before winter.

ACCT Philly - Animal Wellness Resources
Winter Ice and snow bring specific risks for Philadelphia pets. Road salt and ice-melting chemicals can irritate or burn paw pads - rinse your dog's paws after every winter walk. Consider dog booties or a paw balm for protection. Keep cats indoors during cold snaps. Check under your car before starting the engine, as outdoor cats sometimes shelter near warm engines.
